Design system · direction “Cal”

Architectural warmth, tied by a single line.

Cal is the base: warmth delivered through saturated, architectural color planes — never cream, never cozy. From the “Trazo” direction we keep only the idea, recolored into Cal’s warm palette: the mark — two points, one line — is the grammar of the whole product. The two points are always two people; the line ties what is owed to its breakdown, becomes the activity timeline, and closes into a knot at every settlement.

Saturated planes scale because they’re rationed. Rosa is the architecture of human, decisive moments — the Home front door, primary actions, the consent moment — while data, lists, and forms stay calm. Color is architecture; it never touches a number.

Signature — the line

One line, two points, carried everywhere

The brand mark is not decoration — it’s the structural rule. Wherever two things relate (two people, you and an action, you and the person you’re inviting), a line joins two points. Solid = connected/settled. Dashed = pending or not-yet-connected. The line is warm hairline; only an active endpoint takes rosa, only a settled knot takes sage. This is the entire system in one gesture.

03

Patterns

The semantic rules — where things go and why. These bind every screen.

Money rule — colour is architecture, never the number

The rosa plane and accent draw the eye to actions and to the “you owe” chip. Amounts — net, per-pair, per-expense, funds — are always neutral ink. The constraint is where emphasis lands, not what it looks like.

Direction lives in the chip

The +/− sign is dropped from every displayed amount. Who owes whom is read from the DirChip beside the number, never from the number itself.

Never alarm-red

Nothing about money or actions turns red. Errors use a warm, neutral treatment; the “you owe” emphasis is an affordance, not a warning — including FlowWidget errors.

The line system

Two points, one line — carried across the product. Home: the net ties down to its breakdown. Group detail: the timeline is the line, and a settlement is a knot where prior expenses close. Add someone: your point reaches a dashed-open point. FlowWidget: the line draws, then knots (done) or gaps (error).

Timeline structure

The activity list is segmented by settlements. A settlement knot closes the expenses above it; they collapse and expand on tap. The open window sits at the top, newest first. Edited rows carry a badge; deleted rows are gone.

Form-as-screen · one consent per write

Add / edit expense and add someone are full screens (mobile) or dialogs (desktop), never sheets over content. Every data-changing action opens the FlowWidget, where the user taps exactly one confirm. It never auto-fires.

Post-write resilience

After a write succeeds, the list lags briefly. The app shows a calm grey “Saved — updating…”, then “Saved — reload to see the latest.” A failed refresh is never shown as a write failure.
